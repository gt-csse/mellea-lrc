# Root identity resolution generality audit, 2026-09-21

This report records the v49 and v53 experiments. The current pipeline subsequently replaced the experimental opinion-header date repair with a simpler rule: compare complete dates when both are available; if both have at least a year, compare years; if either side lacks a date, make no date judgment. The v53 score below is therefore historical, not a score for the current pipeline.

The current root-identity design has reusable boundaries: source text and provider records, not gold annotation, enter the prompts; docket re-extraction copies from a target-only window; third-party review quotes an independently retrieved citation and grounds it at source offsets. The latest prompts nevertheless have not been evaluated on a matching non-primary identity run. Primary v49 is a development result, not evidence that the resolution mechanism generalizes.

## Prompt and decision-contract findings

1. **The latest root-identity prompts do not contain a hard-coded primary case example, but repeated primary tuning can still overfit the decision mechanism.** Metadata search asks for short fragments copied from the source case name; docket re-extraction uses a target-only filing window; third-party review receives source context plus retrieved body evidence. No annotation label is passed to those prompts. Their thresholds, branch conditions, and instructions have nevertheless been revised against primary failures without a current external identity score.
2. **Third-party decisions carry an explicit evidence type.** `body_search.py` returns separate locator, case-name, court, and date judgments. It admits `corroborates` when the locator matches and the other fields do not affirmatively mismatch, and marks the resulting identity judgment `third_party`. This evidence is qualified by that type rather than by a stronger admission gate. In saved v49 third-party reporter admissions, 11/11 source and cited digit sequences agree. One correct admission has `unavailable` court and date comparisons because the source states neither. The transferred reliability of these decisions remains to be measured on other corpora.
3. **The direct body route is less granular than third-party review.** A unique self-identifying metadata hit with an exactly normalized case name can resolve programmatically. Its court check rejects only a present conflicting court, and its date check uses a decision year or docket-filing chronology. The model path asks for a candidate index and copied identifier, not independent court/date judgments. Three saved v49 admissions use this direct route without the side-by-side field-comparison trace. They are gold-correct in primary, but a full date written in another corpus would not be checked at its stated precision by this route.
4. **Exact reporter date and trace wording were corrected after this audit.** The exact opinion route now compares a stated month/day/year against the provider's complete opinion date, deferring conflicts to opinion-header date review. A retrieved year alone is insufficient to verify a full cited date. Docket filing dates remain chronology evidence. Candidate choice still selects an index or `no_match`; its resolution trace no longer claims an unconditional reparse, and its context now exposes any separately reviewed local case name. Missing court/date evidence can still be noncontradictory without confirming that field.
5. **Some name semantics are too narrow.** The semantic case-name prompt says both sides of `v.` must be represented, although the deterministic name check accepts single-party `In re` and `Ex parte` captions. A valid variant that needs semantic review can therefore be called a mismatch. The 40% docket-number shortlist and the 20-candidate review boundary are retrieval/cost decisions, not identity evidence; their sensitivity across docket formats and court sizes has not been measured.
6. **Primary-derived site-review examples were removed after this audit.** `extraction/adjudication/review/case_name.py` now states the general rules without named examples from primary. The case-name search query prompt uses invented examples. `tests/test_prompt_holdout.py` now scans all five active diagnostic corpora and runtime prefix constants; it runs rather than skipping on the current dataset layout. It deliberately does not read the untouched final held-out set. This static guard catches copied examples, not overfitting through iterative rule tuning.

The sole wrong primary v49 admission is a misspelled case name (*Suffock* versus *Suffolk*) that semantic equivalence accepted. This illustrates the tradeoff between tolerating legitimate caption variants and checking spelling as written; one error does not establish a transferable false-admission rate.

## Exact reporter date caveat

The saved 26-filing primary checkpoint contains 392 full reporter roots. Forty-five state a complete month/day/year, but none returned a unique or ambiguous exact reporter lookup; all 45 exact lookups were `not_found`. The new full-day comparison therefore changes **zero** saved judgments. Eight of those roots were later admitted through independent body citations; 37 remain deferred.

Four year-only unique exact lookups had date-only conflicts. The original v49 opinion-header review resolved one (*Michelson*, whose cited 1948 date appears in a linked opinion header) and left three deferred. Two of the deferred roots are gold-correct amended-opinion citations in filing 019, but their successfully fetched opinion headers contain no usable dated phrase. Date review does not require the cited date to be near the cluster's original date: it compares a full cited date to a full dated event, or a cited year to an event year, and enforces any retrospective cutoff. Its current header scan covers the first 1,400 text characters of up to eight linked opinions. The v49 implementation preserved a deferral when no usable date was found; the scoped rerun below measures the terminal policy that replaced it.

After the user's request for terminal best-effort judgments, a scoped rerun from the saved pre-date checkpoint revised this behavior. The stage now checks matching dates in the opinion header and cluster `other_dates`, refreshing the same exact cluster when older checkpoints omitted that metadata. If neither supports the stated date, it issues `no_match` with `judgement.type = "best_effort_date"` and an explicit caveat that an unrecorded later version could exist. In the scoped 005/019 rerun, *Walker* becomes `no_match` (gold wrong), *Graham-Sult* becomes `resolved` from refreshed `Amended Feb. 5, 2014` metadata (gold correct), and *Ballou* becomes `no_match` (gold correct, hence a false negative). The separate 2022 amended *Ballou* opinion is indexed under a different CourtListener docket record with the same docket number; the current date stage does not search sibling docket records. A model-only alternative falsely admitted *Walker* by treating the year inside a parallel LEXIS identifier as decision-date evidence, so it was removed. The remaining *Ballou* error is a concrete reason to consider a generalized related-opinion search when retrieval quota permits, rather than adding a direction-of-year heuristic.

The full 26-filing rerun is saved at `data/run-artifacts/53-primary-date-refresh-full-v1/full_reporter_exact_date_review` and scored in `primary-v53-root-identity-score.json`. Relative to v49, gold-correct admissions rise from 288/366 to 289/366, and decided roots rise from 324/440 to 327/440. Correct decisions rise from 317 to 319, while false rejections rise from six to seven because of *Ballou*. This is a small net gain with a known, consequential miss; it does not establish that terminal date review is reliable across opinion versions. The 45 complete-date roots with `not_found` exact reporter lookups are a separate group: `not_found` means that endpoint did not identify a cluster, not that CourtListener has no opinion cluster reachable by later search.
