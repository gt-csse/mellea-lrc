The District defends its assignment plan on a single ground: that the two
campuses it operates receive identical funding, identical staffing, and
identical facilities, so no student is disadvantaged by being sent to one rather
than the other. That defense is foreclosed. Segregating public school children
by race denies them equal educational opportunity even when the physical
facilities and other tangible factors at each school are equal. See Brown v.
Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483, 495 (1954). Because the District's entire
justification rests on the equality of tangible resources, and because equality
of those resources is not the measure, the plan cannot stand.
